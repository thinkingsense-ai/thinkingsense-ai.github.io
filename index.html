Ontology & knowledge graph

Define business meaning once, and link records across sources

Definitions, relationships and policy are learned from schemas, sampled values, query logs and documents. The records themselves stay in your systems.

  • Four learning signals, one review queue
  • Nothing inferred goes live without a person
  • Used to resolve terms, apply access and plan queries

Governed agents

Gather facts, check rules, and put a reviewer in charge

An agent can only propose from the actions your policy allows. A named person approves, and every step lands in a tamper-evident record.

  • Same firewall and row rules as a person
  • Dry run before any write-back
  • Workflow API, commercial edition
Read the agents guide →
Small-model tuning

Train for one repeatable task, then measure it on unseen cases

Recorded decisions become a dataset, a LoRA adapter is trained and served locally, and a promotion gate compares it with your current path.

  • Scored on matching result rows, not SQL text
  • Local first, with escalation
  • Tooling exists; no tuned model promoted yet

A worked example

From one question to an approved action

A risk team's evidence sits in two databases. This is what changes when one question can reach both, and when the answer can carry on into a governed action.

"Which tier-1 services have both an open critical vulnerability and an outage in the last 90 days?"

Postgres holds services, incidents and the risk register. MariaDB holds vendors and vulnerabilities.
The usual way
  • Ask the data team to join the two systems.
  • Wait for a one-off extract and a spreadsheet.
  • Chase the vendor owner by email to decide what to do.
  • Reconstruct later who approved what, and on which facts.
With ThinkingSense
  • One question, one query across both systems. Payments Processing, Online Banking and Treasury Management come back, each with the sources named.
  • The evidence is gathered for you: a CVSS 9.9 vulnerability 56 days past its patch deadline, a tier-1 vendor last assessed 520 days ago, a 64-minute outage against a 45-minute recovery objective.
  • Your rules decide what is allowed, not the model. Each rule shows whether it held.
  • A person approves. The person who started it cannot, and an AI identity cannot approve at all.
  • The action goes to the system that owns it, and a tamper-evident record shows every step.
About this example. The data is invented and comes from the IT and operational risk sample set, so dates move with the day it is loaded. The workflow is the product's workflow API (commercial edition). Walk through all six steps →
