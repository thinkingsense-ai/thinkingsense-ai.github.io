Quick start
● Solutions

Ask across your systems. Act on the answer.

A solution is a pack: the data model, the questions your team asks, the rules that apply, and, where it fits, a workflow that carries an answer into a governed action. Packs read your systems where they are, with each person's own access, and move nothing into a new store.

Flagship · IT and operational risk

Ask a question. Take an informed action.

A risk team's evidence sits in more than one system. One question reads both where they are, applies the asker's access, and hands the answer to a workflow with named approvers and a tamper-evident record.

"Which tier-1 services have both an open critical vulnerability and an outage in the last 90 days?"

Postgres · services, applications, incidents, risk register MariaDB · vendors, vulnerabilities
  1. 1

    Ask in plain language

    One question, answered by a single query that joins the two systems at the time you ask. It runs with your permissions, so you see only what you are allowed to see.

    Payments Processing · 2 critical open · 178 min of outageOnline Banking · 1 · 238 minTreasury Management · 1 · 79 min
  2. 2

    Gather the evidence for one service

    Pick Treasury Management and the workflow collects the facts from both sources, with the source named beside each.

    Vulnerability · CVE-2026-0871, CVSS 9.9, 56 days past its patch deadlineVendor · Helix Treasury Software, tier 1, last assessed 520 days ago, rated HighOutage · 64 minutes against a 45-minute recovery objectiveRevenue at risk · about $341,000 at $320,000 an hourOpen risk · "Treasury software vendor not assessed in over a year"
  3. 3

    Check it against your rules

    Fixed rules decide what is allowed, not the model: critical and overdue, vendor assessment older than 12 months, outage longer than the recovery objective. Each rule shows whether it held.

  4. 4

    Recommend, within the allowed actions

    The model proposes an action, such as raising a remediation exception with the vendor owner and bringing the vendor assessment forward. It can only choose from the actions your policy permits, and it explains why.

  5. 5

    A person approves

    The approval is tied to the exact facts shown. The person who started the workflow cannot approve it, and an AI identity cannot approve at all. Above a limit, two approvers are required.

  6. 6

    Act, and keep the record

    The action is sent to the system that owns it, or shown as a dry run first. Every step is written to a tamper-evident decision record you can check later.

About this example. The data is invented and the figures come from the IT and operational risk sample set, so the dates move with the day it is loaded. The workflow is the product's workflow API (commercial edition): rules, a recommendation limited to the allowed actions, named approvers and a hash-chained record. The IT-risk definition is built and unit-tested; the figures here were worked out from the sample data, not taken from a recorded run, and the final step is a dry run because the demo has no ticketing tool connected.

The packs

A starting point for each function

Every pack is a working data model with approved questions: SQL written once and checked against the data. The data in all of them is invented. Questions below are word for word from each pack's approved list.

Supply chain

Suppliers, stock, purchase orders and shipments · 5 approved questions

Who asks: Procurement and operations leads

Suppliers, warehouse stock, purchase orders and carriers, as three separate sources that are read together at question time.

  • Which suppliers do we hold the most stock from?
  • How many units are on order from suppliers in each region?
  • Which in-transit purchase orders arrive first, and from which supplier and carrier?

Shows: a plain-English question answered across several sources, with the sources and checks shown under the answer.

IT and operational risk

Business services, outages, risks, controls, vendors and vulnerabilities · 12 approved questions, 4 read more than one system

Who asks: CRO, CIO, CISO and risk teams

Services, applications, incidents, the risk register, appetite and controls in Postgres; third-party vendors and vulnerabilities in MariaDB.

  • Which tier-1 services have both an open critical vulnerability and an outage in the last 90 days?
  • Which critical vulnerabilities are past their patch deadline, and which business service does each affect?
  • Which critical third parties have not been assessed in the last 12 months, and which services do they support?

Shows: a question that joins two different database products, then a workflow that turns the answer into a reviewed action.

BOM and sourcing

Products, assemblies, parts, suppliers, facilities and certificates · 24 approved questions

Who asks: Engineers, buyers and quality teams

Bills of materials, supplier quotes, facility certificates, purchase and work orders, quality records and engineering changes.

  • Which parts are in the KR-6 robot arm, and how many of each does it use?
  • Which material is used by the most parts?
  • Which facilities hold a valid AS9100 certificate today?

Shows: multi-step what-ifs that combine cost, certification and location rules.

Treasury

Cash, payables, hedges, debt and an agent action log · 24 approved questions, 4 read more than one system

Who asks: CFO, treasurer and finance operations

Accounts, balances, FX, payments and policy, hedges, debt and covenants in Postgres; vendors, invoices and the ledger from the ERP in MariaDB.

  • What is our consolidated cash position in US dollars across all open accounts?
  • What is the cash position of each legal entity in US dollars?
  • How much cash is held at each bank, and what share of the total is that?

Shows: one view of cash and obligations that normally sits in separate systems, and a payment-exception workflow with dual approval.

Manufacturing sourcing

Parts, bills of materials and supplier capabilities · 6 approved questions

Who asks: Sourcing and manufacturing engineering

Added to the supply-chain schemas: which parts go into which products, and which suppliers can make them.

  • Which parts in the JM-200 joint module are custom and which are catalog items?
  • Which parts have only one supplier quote, and where is that supplier?
  • Which quotes would miss the need date if we ordered on the quote date?

Shows: how a second pack extends the first without a new integration.

Open data: Smithsonian

A sample of Smithsonian Open Access records and a museum directory · 5 approved questions, 1 read more than one system

Who asks: Anyone exploring a public collection

A sample of CC0 records in Postgres and a museum directory held in a file, so one question can read a database and a file.

  • What do we hold on the American Civil War across all museums, and how much of it has an image?
  • Which museums have the most records with no image?
  • Show me what the Smithsonian has about the Civil War

Shows: a database and a file answered together.

Why it holds up

The same three guarantees under every pack

Where the data stays
Queries run against the systems that own the data. Nothing is copied into a new store, and each source is named beside the result it supplied.
Who sees what
Access is checked before anything runs, as the signed-in person. A workspace can scope a pack to the sources it needs.
Who decides
Rules set what is allowed, a model proposes within them, and a person approves. The starter and any AI identity cannot approve, and every step is recorded.

Workflows are part of the commercial edition. Approved questions are the dependable demonstration; a question in your own words is written by the model each time and is less reliable on multi-table packs.
