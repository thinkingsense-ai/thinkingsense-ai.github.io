Quick start
● Governed agents · technical guide

From an investigation to a reviewed decision

An agent gathers permitted evidence, evaluates rules you set, and prepares a recommendation. A named reviewer makes the approval decision, with the evidence and the proposed action in front of them. This page walks the path and lists what you configure.

Walk the pathTry the click-through demo

This is the technical guide. The overview for your team is at thinkingsense.ai.

The path

Six steps, and where each one is enforced

Shown on the IT and operational risk sample set. The workflow is the product’s workflow API (commercial edition).

  1. 1

    Ask in plain language

    One question, answered by a single query that joins the sources at the time you ask. It runs with the asker’s permissions.

  2. 2

    Gather the evidence

    The workflow collects the facts for one entity from each permitted source, with the source named beside each fact.

  3. 3

    Check it against your rules

    Fixed rules decide what is allowed, not the model. Each rule shows whether it held.

  4. 4

    Recommend, within the allowed actions

    The model proposes an action, but can only choose from the actions your policy permits, and it explains why.

  5. 5

    A person approves

    The approval is tied to the exact facts shown. The person who started the workflow cannot approve it, and an AI identity cannot approve at all. Above a limit, two approvers are required.

  6. 6

    Act, and keep the record

    The action goes to the system that owns it, or is shown as a dry run first. Every step is written to a tamper-evident decision record.

What you configure

Three decisions, made by your team

01

Where the agent looks

Approved sources, entity mappings, and the identity used to reach each system. Scope the first workflow to the records it needs.

02

What it can propose

The rules, the allowed actions, and the information a reviewer needs to judge an exception.

03

Who can approve

Review groups, separation of duties, and any requirement for more than one approver.

Guardrails

Why the model cannot act on its own

01

Same path for AI and people

An agent connecting over MCP goes through the same firewall, routing and rate limits as a person’s session, under the same row and column rules.

02

Choice, not authority

The model picks from a list your policy defines. It cannot invent an action, and it cannot approve one.

03

Dry run first

Start by simulating the action and reviewing exactly what would be sent. Enable write-back only after access, approval and reversal are validated.

On a schedule

Agents that run without being asked

Scheduled agents run on a trigger, query the data, summarize, and deliver the result by email or Slack. An admin can approve or flag an individual past run to vouch for that exact output. Proactive insights re-check approved questions and flag material changes.
