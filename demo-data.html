Quick start
● Every record is invented

Try ThinkingSense on ready-made data

A new deployment comes with demo data and approved questions, so you can ask a real question in the first minute. Pick one of five sets when you deploy. Each is a small, made-up business spread over more than one system, so the questions show how ThinkingSense reads across them.

Choosing one

Five sets, one choice at deploy time

The choice is the Demo data setting on the AWS, Oracle Cloud and Azure forms, and demo_dataset on Google Cloud. The sets replace each other (the free edition allows three data sources), and the choice is read on a fresh install only, so changing it later does not reseed. All packs is the default on AWS; pick one pack on the form if you want a lighter start.

The packs

What is in each, and what to ask

The questions below are word for word from each set's approved list.

Supply chain

supply-chain · suppliers, inventory and procurement as three separate sources

A small manufacturer's suppliers, warehouse stock, purchase orders and shipments. Three of these five questions read across two or three of the sources; the other two stay in one.

  • Which suppliers do we hold the most stock from?
  • How many units are on order from suppliers in each region?
  • Which in-transit purchase orders arrive first, and from which supplier and carrier?
  • How many shipments does each carrier have?
  • How much stock is in each warehouse?

Shows: a plain-English question answered across several sources, with the sources and checks listed under the answer.

IT and operational risk

it-risk · a mid-size financial firm · Postgres and MariaDB

Business services, outages, risks and controls, key risk indicators, third-party assessments and audit issues in one database, and the vendors and their vulnerabilities in another.

  • Which business services had an outage longer than their recovery time objective in the last 12 months?
  • What are the leading root causes of outages, by total downtime?
  • Which risks are above the board's risk appetite for their category?
  • Which critical vulnerabilities are past their patch deadline, and which business service does each affect? reads both systems
  • Which tier-1 services have both an open critical vulnerability and an outage in the last 90 days? reads both systems

Shows: a question that needs a join across two different database products, which a single-database tool cannot answer.

BOM and sourcing

bom · manufacturing · one Postgres source with 22 tables

Products, assemblies and parts, suppliers and quotes, facilities and their certificates and machines, purchase orders, work orders, quality records, engineering changes and design-for-manufacture findings.

  • Which parts are in the KR-6 robot arm, and how many of each does it use?
  • Which parts have only one supplier quote, and which facility is it from?
  • Which facilities hold a valid AS9100 certificate today?
  • A fire hit our Shenzhen plant. Which parts do we currently source there, and which products do they go into?
  • If we moved every SK-9 custom part that has a compliant North American source to it, how would the SK-9 unit BOM cost change? a what-if

Shows: the business questions an engineer or buyer asks, including multi-step what-ifs that combine cost, certification and location rules.

Treasury

treasury · cash, payables, hedges and debt · Postgres and the ERP

Accounts, balances, FX rates, payments and payment policy, hedges, debt and covenants, a log of what a treasury agent did, and the ERP's vendors, invoices and ledger.

  • What is our consolidated cash position in US dollars across all open accounts?
  • Which payments are waiting for approval, and how much is each in US dollars?
  • What did the treasury agent do outside policy in the last 30 days?
  • Which payments waiting for approval are for invoices that are on hold? reads both systems
  • For each foreign currency, how much of our open payables is covered by active hedges? reads both systems

Shows: one view of cash and obligations that normally sits in separate systems, and the controls around payments (policy limits, second approvers, agent actions).

All packs

all-packs · six packs at once · a picker in the Ask app

Supply chain, manufacturing, IT and operational risk, BOM and sourcing, treasury, and a sample of the Smithsonian Open Access collection (records, with images where they exist) in one deployment, behind the free edition's three data sources. The Ask app offers the packs as a picker so each starts from its own questions.

  • Show me what the Smithsonian has about the Civil War
  • Which museums have the most records with no image?
  • How many records does each museum hold, with its full name and department?

Shows: a realistic mix of sources in one place. It is the heaviest choice and needs the most memory (on AWS the default node size ran it, with little headroom).

If you just installed it

Your first ten minutes

1

Open the Ask app and sign in

Use the address from your deployment's outputs (AskAppUrl on AWS) and the login you chose at deploy time. Your browser will warn about the certificate the first time, because the stack makes its own; continue past it.

2

Pick a pack

With all packs installed, each pack is a Workspace you choose from the picker: supply chain, manufacturing, IT and operational risk, BOM, Treasury and the Smithsonian sample. The Workspace decides which data a question can use.

3

Click an approved question

Approved questions are written once and checked against the data, so they are the dependable way to see what the product does. The answer shows the rows, a chart where one fits, and the sources and checks used, listed under it.

4

Try a question that reads two systems

In Treasury, ask which payments waiting for approval are for invoices that are on hold. It joins a Postgres database to the ERP in MariaDB, which a single-database tool cannot do.

5

Then ask in your own words

This is written by the model each time, and on the multi-table packs it is less reliable than an approved question. It needs the Anthropic API key you gave at deploy time. It is the fair way to see how far it gets.

6

Look behind the answer

Sign in to the admin console at /admin on the same address with the admin login: data sources, the approved-question bank and the ontology live there. Every record is invented, so nothing you do can touch real data.

How questions work here

Approved questions and your own

Each set ships with approved questions: questions with SQL that was written once and checked against the data. In the Ask app they appear as starters. On BOM, treasury and all-packs the deployment also runs a small embedding model that matches what you type to an approved question, so asking one as written returns the saved, checked answer. On supply chain and IT risk they are starters to click.

Your own wording is a different story. A question that is not an approved one is written by the model each time, and on these multi-table sets it is noticeably less reliable than an approved question, with answers that are sometimes wrong or declined. Treat approved questions as the dependable demonstration and free-form questions as a way to see how far it gets. Measured results for each set are in the READMEs linked below.
Optional

Agency: risk management on the same data

Agency is a second front end beside the Ask app, with its own address and the same logins. Turn it on in the deploy form (Install Agency beside the Ask app). It adds the IT-risk investigation data and needs about 1 GiB more memory, so use nodes of 8 GiB or more.

  • Watch lists events in the news under review (an outage, a breach, a claim that AI was behind it). An optional pod reads 12 public feeds every 30 minutes; it keeps headlines and links only.
  • Exposure asks five questions of a simulated company estate and gives one rating: Low, Medium, High or Critical, naming the questions that set it.
  • Priorities ranks the estate's systems by that rating and the reasons, and Notifications shows regulator deadlines once a person has recorded that an incident is major. Nothing is ever sent to a regulator.
  • Problem records trace an incident from symptom to cause, grading each link by its evidence, and a read-only Quickstart tour walks through it.
Workflows. Sending a record for approval, the approvals inbox and the audit view run on the workflow API, which is part of the commercial edition at present. In the free edition you can read everything above but not start or approve a run. Write-back is always a dry run: nothing is sent to a real system.

Without a cloud account

Run them on your own machine

Each set also runs with Docker, from a clone of the Docker repository; you need your own Anthropic API key. The default (supply chain) is the repository's docker-compose.yml. The others:

docker compose --project-directory . -f demos/bom-pack/docker-compose.bom.yml up --build
docker compose --project-directory . -f demos/treasury-pack/docker-compose.treasury.yml up --build
docker compose -f docker-compose.yml -f demos/all-packs/docker-compose.packs.yml up --build
